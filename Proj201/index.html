01_INSTRUCTIONS · Project Handbook & Guidelines

Why Do Brain Drugs Fail? Clinical Successes and Failures in Alzheimer's, Glioblastoma, and Stroke.

Core Objective: Conduct a systematic, evidence-based investigation of clinical trials to understand why brain therapeutics fail and what lessons emerge for future drug development.
1
Build Reliable Database

Collect 10–15 well-documented Phase II/III trials per disease (30–45 total) with traceable DOIs/PMIDs.

2
Understand Outcomes

Distinguish documented reasons for negative outcomes from hypothesized translational contributing factors.

3
Identify Future Lessons

Examine Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) delivery, disease heterogeneity, patient selection, and trial endpoints.

7. Summary Comparison of Common and Disease-Specific Challenges

Disease Primary Biological Barriers Recurring Failure Mechanisms Key Translational Lessons
Alzheimer's Blood-Brain Barrier restriction for mAbs (~0.1%); Synaptic loss precedes clinical diagnosis. Intervening too late (moderate dementia); off-target catalytic toxicity (gamma-secretase); ARIA edema. Biomarker-guided early screening is critical; clearance of soluble protofibrils slows decline modestly.
GBM Intact blood-tumor barrier at invasive margins; dense myeloid immunosuppressive microenvironment. Subclonal tumor antigen escape (loss of target mutations like EGFRvIII); cold immunophenotype. Single-antigen monotherapy rapidly fails against clonal heterogeneity; polyvalent/local delivery needed.
Stroke Strict therapeutic window (<4.5 hours); ischemic cascade triggers secondary hemorrhagic transformation. Polar BBB impermeability in penumbra; lack of recanalization; enzymatic drug-drug degradation. "Time is brain": neuroprotectants cannot salvage tissue without reperfusion; early timing is paramount.

Categories A & B: Identification & Treatment

Trial Phase: Phase I, Phase I–II, Phase II, Phase II–III, Phase III, Phase IV
Study Status: Completed, Terminated
Therapeutic Approach: Small molecule, Antibody, Biologic, Cell therapy, Other
Administration Route: Oral, Intravenous, Intrathecal, Intranasal, Other
Treatment Objective: Disease-modifying, Symptomatic, Neuroprotective, Other

Categories C & D: Design & Clinical Outcome

Study Design: Randomized, Non-randomized
Control Group: Placebo, Standard treatment, Active comparator, None
Primary Endpoint Result: Met, Not met, Inconclusive, Not reported
Overall Clinical Outcome:
  • Positive: Prespecified primary endpoint met according to reported evidence.
  • Negative: Primary endpoint not met or lack of efficacy documented.
  • Mixed / Inconclusive: Mixed results or conflicting clinical endpoints.
  • Not assessable: Insufficient publicly accessible outcome information.
